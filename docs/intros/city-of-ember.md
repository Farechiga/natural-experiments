# The City of Ember

**Narrator:** Imagine a city where morning means someone switches on the lamps. There is no sunrise. Now imagine those lamps starting to fail.

**Audrey:** Wait. What is the backup plan?

**Narrator:** That's the trouble, Audrey. In Jeanne DuPrau's The City of Ember, people depend on an aging generator. Beyond their streets lies darkness.

**Narrator:** Lina Mayfleet is twelve, old enough here to leave school and start work. She wants to be a messenger, running through Ember with news to deliver.

**Paxten:** So this frightening place is also the place she loves.

**Narrator:** It is, Paxten. Her little sister lives here. So does everyone she knows. But Lina keeps drawing another city, filled with light.

**Narrator:** Doon Harrow wants to work down in the Pipeworks, near the generator. He wants to understand what is breaking, and do something that matters.

**Audrey:** Knowing something is broken is easier than knowing how to fix it.

**Narrator:** Doon will discover just how much harder. He and Lina trade jobs, each getting the work they wanted.

**Narrator:** Then Lina finds a damaged message. Words are missing. The scraps might contain something Ember desperately needs.

**Paxten:** And they need the lights to stay on long enough to read it.

**Narrator:** Together, they start piecing it together while the blackouts grow longer.

**Narrator:** But if the clues lead beyond the streets they know, how do you take the first step into the dark?
